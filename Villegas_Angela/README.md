# Interpretation Questions

1. What biological question was the original RNA-seq study trying to answer?

The study aimed to determine how *Epinephelus coioides* responds physiologically and at the gene-expression level to acute hypoxia and reoxygenation. It also investigated differences between hypoxia-tolerant and hypoxia-sensitive fish.

2. Why did the authors use RNA-seq instead of only examining the genome?

RNA-seq allows researchers to examine which genes are actively expressed under different conditions. The genome contains the organism's DNA, but RNA-seq shows changes in gene activity in response to hypoxia.

3. What is the difference between genomic DNA and the RNA molecules measured by RNA-seq?

Genomic DNA contains the organism's complete genetic information and is relatively stable across cells. The RNA molecules measured by RNA-seq represent genes that are being actively transcribed in a particular tissue or condition.

4. What is a biological replicate and why is it important?

A biological replicate is an independent biological sample from the same experimental condition, such as a different fish. Replicates help determine whether observed differences are consistent and biologically meaningful rather than caused by individual variation.

5. What is the difference between single-end and paired-end sequencing?

Single-end sequencing reads each DNA fragment from only one end. Paired-end sequencing reads both ends of the same fragment, providing additional information that can improve sequence alignment and transcript analysis.

6. What is a FASTQ file?

A FASTQ file contains nucleotide sequences generated by sequencing together with their quality scores. Each read is represented by sequence information and a corresponding quality value for each base.

7. What information does FastQC provide?

FastQC provides quality-control information about sequencing data, including read quality, GC content, sequence length, adapter contamination, and overrepresented sequences. It helps identify possible problems with the raw RNA-seq reads before further analysis.

8. What does a high per-base quality score indicate?

A high per-base quality score indicates that the sequencing instrument has high confidence in the accuracy of that nucleotide call. Therefore, the corresponding base is less likely to contain a sequencing error.

9. Why can adapter contamination be a problem?

Adapter contamination occurs when sequencing adapters are included in the reads instead of only the biological sequence. It can interfere with read alignment and downstream RNA-seq analyses, so adapters may need to be removed during quality control.

10. Were all RNA-seq samples in your group similar in quality? Explain.

The samples were generally comparable in overall sequencing quality, but some FastQC modules showed different results between samples. For example, some samples had failures in per-base sequence content, GC content, or sequence duplication levels.

11. Did any sample show a possible quality problem? What was it?

Yes, some samples showed possible quality issues based on FastQC warnings or failures. The main concerns included per-base sequence content, per-sequence GC content, and sequence duplication levels, depending on the sample and read direction.

12. What additional steps would be needed before the researchers could compare gene expression between control and treatment samples?

The reads would first need to undergo quality control and preprocessing, followed by adapter and low-quality read trimming and alignment or transcript quantification. The resulting gene-expression counts would then be normalized and analyzed statistically for differential expression between control and treatment groups.
